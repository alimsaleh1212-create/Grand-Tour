# Dubai — City of Superlatives
*Style: Luxury | Region: Middle East*

Dubai is the largest city in the United Arab Emirates, a purpose-built metropolis that has transformed from a pearl-diving port into one of the world's wealthiest cities within a single generation. It holds the record for the world's tallest building (Burj Khalifa, 828 m), the largest indoor ski slope, the world's biggest shopping mall, and the most expensive hotel room. The city makes excess its identity and delivers on the promise with extraordinary infrastructure, impeccable service standards, and a genuinely stunning skyline.

## The Luxury Experience

Dubai's luxury offering is legitimately world-class. The Burj Al Arab (sail-shaped island hotel, from USD 1,200/night) is one of the world's most recognisable buildings and functions as a city landmark as much as an accommodation. Downtown Dubai, centred on the Burj Khalifa and Dubai Fountain, concentrates designer retail, Michelin-starred restaurants, and rooftop bars with views that justify their prices.

**Burj Khalifa** — The observation deck (levels 124 and 148) offers views across the entire Gulf coastline on clear days. Timed entry USD 35–65.

**Palm Jumeirah** — The artificial palm-shaped archipelago houses the Atlantis resort, five-star beach hotels (One&Only, W Dubai, Waldorf Astoria), and private beachfront villas. The Palm Monorail connects to the city.

**Dubai Mall and Gold Souk** — The mall houses an aquarium, an ice rink, and 1,300 shops. The traditional Gold Souk in Deira is one of the world's largest gold markets; prices are transparent and negotiable.

**Desert safaris** — Day and overnight desert experiences (dune bashing in 4x4, camel riding, Bedouin camp dinner with entertainment) from USD 60–300. The Liwa Oasis and Al Maha Desert Resort offer premium desert stays.

**Indoor ski slope (Ski Dubai)** — 22,500 sq m of real snow inside a mall. Day pass USD 90. Surreal and genuinely fun.

## Practical Information

**Getting there:** Dubai International Airport is one of the world's busiest (Terminals 1 and 3; Terminal 2 for budget carriers). Emirates hub; well-connected to Europe, Asia, Africa, and Australia. Dubai Metro connects the airport to Downtown in 30 minutes.

**Cost per day:** USD 200–600+ mid-to-high end. Budget travel is possible (hostel dorms from USD 20; shawarma meals USD 3–5) but the city's identity is luxury. Mid-range hotel USD 120–300; restaurant meal USD 30–80; cocktail at a rooftop bar USD 20–35.

**Safety:** 8.5/10. Extremely low crime (strong rule of law). Public intoxication, drug possession, and certain forms of public affection are illegal; tourist arrests are rare but headline-generating when they occur.

**Language:** Arabic officially; English is a practical lingua franca for business and tourism. All major signs are bilingual.

**Best seasons:** November–April (20–28°C, low humidity). May–October is extremely hot (35–48°C) with high humidity; this period sees 30–50% hotel price drops and fewer tourists.

## Food Scene

Dubai's restaurant scene is legitimately world-class — driven by the city's expat population (88% of residents are foreign nationals) and high disposable incomes. Zuma, Nobu, Nusr-Et, and Pierchic (overwater restaurant) are landmarks. The Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood has authentic Emirati dining at fraction of Downtown prices.

## Cultural Considerations

The UAE is a Muslim country. During Ramadan (dates vary), eating/drinking in public during daylight is illegal for everyone, including non-Muslims; restaurants operate with reduced hours. Dress modestly outside beach/hotel areas; abaya required to enter mosques.
