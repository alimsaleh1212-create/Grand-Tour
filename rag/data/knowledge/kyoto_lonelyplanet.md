# Kyoto Travel Guide — Practical Visitor Notes
*Style: Culture | Region: Asia | Source: Lonely Planet*

## Why Kyoto

No city in Japan concentrates so much history into a single day's walk. Kyoto was spared Allied bombing in World War II specifically because of its cultural significance, leaving its wooden architecture and shrine complexes intact. A full week here barely scratches the surface; a long weekend is enough to understand why it earns superlatives from every travel writer who visits.

## Must-See Sights

**Arashiyama** — The bamboo grove and Tenryu-ji's moss garden anchor the western hills district. Arrive at 6:30 a.m. for the bamboo path with minimal crowds. Okochi Sanso villa (¥1,000 with matcha) is the hidden gem most guides underrate.

**Fushimi Inari** — The mountain of orange gates is Kyoto's most photographed sight. Most visitors stop at the first ridge (30 min); the full summit circuit (4 hours) reaches empty upper shrines with city views.

**Kinkaku-ji and Ginkaku-ji** — The Golden and Silver Pavilions bookend Kyoto's two great garden traditions. Visit both on the same day via Philosopher's Path.

**Nijo Castle** — The 1626 shogun's palace has "nightingale floors" that squeak as you walk, designed to alert guards to intruders. The audience chambers retain their original painted sliding panels.

## Getting Around

Kyoto's bus network covers 95% of sights; the ¥700 all-day pass pays for itself after three rides. Bicycle rental (¥1,000/day) is the ideal transport for the northern and eastern districts. The subway has two lines and is useful for Nijo Castle and Kyoto Station connections.

## Neighbourhoods

**Higashiyama** — The preserved hillside district between Kiyomizu-dera and Chion-in. Cobblestone lanes, ceramic shops, matcha cafes. Best on weekday mornings.

**Gion** — The geisha district. Hanamikoji Street is the postcard; the parallel Shirakawa canal and Tatsumi Bridge are quieter and equally atmospheric.

**Fushimi** — South of downtown, home to Inari shrine and several sake breweries open for tasting.

## Costs

Mid-range day budget: ¥12,000–20,000 (USD 80–135). Accommodation drives costs most; a machiya townhouse costs ¥8,000–15,000/night. Temple and shrine admissions run ¥500–1,000 each. Meals in Gion restaurants ¥1,500–4,000. Street food and convenience store options from ¥300.

## When to Go

Peak seasons (cherry blossom: late March to early April; autumn colour: mid-November) are crowded and require advance accommodation booking. January and February are cold but uncrowded; temples receive light dustings of snow that make for extraordinary photography.

## Cultural Notes

Remove shoes before entering tatami rooms; this applies to temples, ryokan, and many traditional restaurants. Geisha photography from a respectful distance is acceptable on Hanamikoji; entering private alleys to photograph geisha is not. Tipping is not customary in Japan and may cause confusion.
