# Dubai — Visitor's Practical Guide
*Style: Luxury | Region: Middle East | Source: Lonely Planet*

## Understanding Dubai

Dubai is a city that demands you abandon preconceptions. It is simultaneously a deeply conservative Islamic culture and a city where champagne is served at a rooftop infinity pool overlooking a mosque. It is deeply artificial (the Palm was built from nothing; the Burj Khalifa stands in what was desert 25 years ago) and genuinely impressive. The best way to visit is to accept it on its own terms — a monument to what capital and ambition can build — rather than compare it to ancient European cities or Asian cultural capitals.

## The Must-Do List

**Burj Khalifa at Sunset** — Book the At the Top observation deck (level 124) 2 weeks ahead; the 90-minute timed entry includes sunset and the transition to the dazzling light show over the fountain.

**Dubai Fountain** — Free to watch from the boardwalk. Choreographed to operatic music every 30 minutes from 6 p.m.; best views from the terrace of the Armani Hotel.

**Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood (Bastakiya)** — The only surviving traditional Arabic residential district. Wind-tower architecture, the Dubai Museum, and the Majlis Gallery. A 2-hour visit; free to enter the neighbourhood.

**Desert Safari** — Non-negotiable. Book through a reputable operator (Platinum Heritage uses vintage Land Rovers; Arabian Adventures is the mainstream option). Dune bashing at sunset, camel ride, Bedouin camp, falconry demonstration. USD 80–250.

**Gold and Spice Souks** — Take the 1-dirham abra (water taxi) across the Creek from Bur Dubai to Deira. The Gold Souk's 350 shops and the adjacent Spice Souk with its sacks of saffron and frankincense feel entirely removed from the Dubai of towers and malls.

**Burj Al Arab Tour** — If not staying here, book afternoon tea (USD 150/person) for access to the lobby, the SKYVIEW Bar, and the gold-plated interiors. It is an experience, not merely a meal.

## Where to Stay

**Budget (USD 50–120):** XVA Art Hotel (Al Fahidi, boutique, charming courtyard), HiDubai Hostel (modern, well-run), Ibis World Trade Centre.

**Mid-range (USD 150–350):** Premier Inn (great locations), Address Sky View, Vida Downtown Dubai.

**Luxury (USD 400–1,500+):** Burj Al Arab, Atlantis The Palm, One&Only Royal Mirage (Arabian-style, quieter beach location), Four Seasons Resort at Jumeirah Beach.

## Eating Well Without Breaking the Budget

The mistake is eating only in hotel restaurants. The city's large South Asian population has created outstanding value Indian and Pakistani restaurants in Bur Dubai and Deira (biryani USD 5; full thali USD 8). Arabic shawarma from street stands costs USD 2–4. Budget a mid-range restaurant splurge for one of the Friday brunches — a Dubai institution where USD 90–150 buys unlimited food and beverages at a five-star property for 3 hours.

## Getting Around

**Dubai Metro** — Red Line (airport to Marina) and Green Line (Deira/Bur Dubai). Clean, air-conditioned, reliable; single trip from AED 2–8.5 (USD 0.50–2.30). Not a comprehensive network — major attractions spread across a 90-km corridor.

**Taxis** — Metered, cheap by luxury-city standards (airport to Downtown ~USD 15), and plentiful except during rain (yes, it does occasionally rain).

**Uber/Careem:** Available and often cheaper than taxis for shorter rides.

## Safety and Legal Notes

Dubai is very safe for tourists — the crime rate is one of the lowest globally. The legal system is strict. Specifics for tourists: alcohol only in licensed venues (hotels, restaurants with licences); photographing government buildings, military sites, or people without consent is inadvisable; drugs carry mandatory minimum sentences including for trace amounts.
