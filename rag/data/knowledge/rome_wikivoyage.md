# Rome — The Eternal City
*Style: Culture | Region: Europe*

Rome has been continuously inhabited for approximately 2,800 years. It was the capital of the most powerful empire in Western history, then the seat of the Catholic Church, and today is one of the world's most visited cities. The density of historically significant monuments is unmatched: the Colosseum, the Roman Forum, the Pantheon, the Vatican, and Castel Sant'Angelo are all within 4 km of each other. A serious engagement with Roman history and art could occupy months; most visitors manage a rich week.

## Essential Sights

**The Colosseum** — The 50,000-capacity amphitheatre (80 CE) is the world's most famous ancient monument. Book tickets 3–4 weeks ahead for timed entry. Skip-the-line tour guides explain the gladiatorial logistics in detail that signs cannot. USD 18 standard entry; combined ticket includes Roman Forum and Palatine Hill.

**Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel** — 7 km of galleries culminating in Michelangelo's ceiling (1508–1512). Book first-entry tickets (8 a.m. open) online; the chapel becomes uncomfortably crowded by 10 a.m. Vatican Museum entry USD 20. St Peter's Basilica is free; the cupola climb (USD 8 without lift, USD 10 with) offers the best Rome panorama.

**Pantheon** — The world's best-preserved ancient Roman building (125 CE). The unreinforced concrete dome with its open oculus remains an engineering mystery. Admission USD 5. Visit weekday mornings to avoid tour groups.

**Roman Forum and Palatine Hill** — The political and religious centre of ancient Rome. Included in Colosseum ticket. The Via Sacra, the Arch of Titus, and the Temple of Vesta are the anchors; allow 2–3 hours.

**Borghese Gallery** — Rome's most concentrated art collection in a spectacular setting: Bernini's marble sculptures (Pluto and Proserpina, Apollo and Daphne) and Caravaggio paintings in a 17th-century villa. Timed entry (2-hour slots) must be booked weeks ahead. USD 22.

**Trastevere** — The neighbourhood west of the Tiber that most resembles what Rome looked like before mass tourism. Evening cobblestone wandering, trattorias with outdoor tables, street music. Enter via the Ponte Sisto footbridge from Campo de' Fiori.

## Practical Information

**Getting there:** Rome has two airports — Fiumicino (FCO, Leonardo da Vinci) for major carriers and Ciampino (CIA) for budget carriers. FCO to Termini by Leonardo Express (32 min, USD 18) or bus (50 min, USD 7).

**Getting around:** Walk the historic centre (most sights within 30 minutes of each other). Metro has only two relevant lines; trams and buses fill gaps. Taxi or Uber for cross-city trips.

**Cost per day:** USD 100–180 mid-range. Accommodation USD 60–150 (hostel dorm USD 25); meals USD 15–40 (tourist traps near Trevi charge 3× for the same food found 2 streets away). Museum admissions add USD 20–40 on busy days.

**Language:** Italian. English widely spoken in tourist areas; menu translation generally available.

**Safety:** 7.5/10. Petty theft (pickpocketing on buses, crowded sights, Trastevere nights) is the main risk. Keep bags front-facing; avoid the Metro at night.

**Best seasons:** April–May and October–November for mild weather (18–22°C) and manageable crowds. June–August is hot (30–35°C) and very crowded; July–August is busiest. January–February is uncrowded; some sites have shorter hours.

## Food

Roman cuisine is built on simplicity and quality: cacio e pepe (pasta, pecorino, black pepper), carbonara (guanciale, egg, pecorino — no cream), coda alla vaccinara (oxtail stew), supplì (fried rice balls). Gelato is ubiquitous; genuine artisan gelato (look for covered containers, not neon colours in huge mounds) from Giolitti or Fatamorgana.
