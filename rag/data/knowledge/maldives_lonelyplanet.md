# Maldives — Honest Visitor Guide
*Style: Relaxation | Region: Asia | Source: Lonely Planet*

## The Reality of a Maldives Trip

The Maldives ranks consistently among the world's most desired destinations and among its most expensive. Understanding what you're buying is essential before booking: a Maldives holiday is fundamentally a resort package on a private island. There are no cities to explore, no museums, no street food scenes, no overland routes between destinations. The value proposition is simple — exceptional natural beauty, ultra-clear water, and a calibrated resort experience.

## Choosing an Atoll

The archipelago's 26 atolls spread over 750 km north to south. Resort quality varies less by atoll than by price tier. Considerations:

**North Malé Atoll** — Closest to the airport; speedboat transfers 20–45 min. Best for short trips. Dense resort cluster; prices vary widely.

**South Malé Atoll** — World-class surf breaks (Chickens, Cokes). Best for surfers.

**Ari Atoll** — Famous for whale shark sightings year-round and manta rays. Seaplane-only access (30 min); adds USD 600+ per person round-trip.

**Baa Atoll** — UNESCO Biosphere Reserve; Hanifaru Bay sees manta ray aggregations of 200+ individuals June–November. Suitable for snorkellers and divers.

## The Budget vs. Luxury Split

**Five-star resorts (80% of the market):**
- All-inclusive packages USD 400–1,500+ per night for two
- Overwater bungalows or beach villas
- Full water sports, spa, multiple restaurants
- Seaplane transfers often add USD 500–800/person

**Local island guesthouses (the budget option):**
- Maafushi, Thoddoo, Guraidhoo, Dhigurah are the main hubs
- Guesthouses USD 70–150/night
- Bikini beaches designated for tourists; alcohol restricted on local islands
- Real village life, local ferries, authentic fish curry
- Day trips to nearby reefs from USD 25

## Water Experiences

**Snorkelling** is the Maldives' democratic activity — house reefs at most resorts require no skills, equipment available at no extra cost, and marine life is extraordinary. Turtles, reef sharks (blacktip, whitetip), eagle rays, and colourful reef fish are standard encounters.

**Scuba diving** — The Maldives is consistently ranked among the world's top 5 dive destinations. Thilas (submerged reef pinnacles bathed in current) concentrate fish. Dive packages USD 80–130 per dive.

**Whale sharks** — South Ari Atoll has the world's most reliable whale shark aggregation outside seasonal patterns; sightings are possible 12 months a year.

## Practical Advice

**Booking:** Book 4–6 months ahead for peak season (December–April); many resorts sell out. Many rates are non-refundable.

**Dietary needs:** Resorts accommodate dietary restrictions well with advance notice. Local island guesthouses primarily serve fish-based Maldivian food.

**Currency:** USD is accepted everywhere in resorts. Local island shops use Maldivian Rufiyaa.

**Connectivity:** Resort WiFi is functional for communication; speeds vary. Some resorts charge for WiFi.

## Who Should Go

The Maldives is for honeymooners, couples celebrating milestones, serious divers, whale shark enthusiasts, and anyone who wants to lie on an extraordinary beach. It is not for travellers who want culture, cuisine diversity, city life, adventure sports, or strict budgets. The experience is extraordinary within its lane — but the lane is narrow.
